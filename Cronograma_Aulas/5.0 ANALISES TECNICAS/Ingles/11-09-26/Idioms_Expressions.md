# Idioms & Everyday Expressions

## Common Idioms

### Break a leg

- Meaning: Good luck (used before performances)
- Example: "Break a leg in your presentation today!"

### Piece of cake

- Meaning: Very easy
- Example: "That test was a piece of cake."

### It's up to you

- Meaning: The decision is yours
- Example: "We can go to the restaurant or stay home. It's up to you."

### Under the weather

- Meaning: Feeling sick
- Example: "I'm feeling under the weather today."

### Once in a blue moon

- Meaning: Very rarely
- Example: "I eat pizza once in a blue moon."

### Cost an arm and a leg

- Meaning: Very expensive
- Example: "That car cost an arm and a leg."

### Hit the nail on the head

- Meaning: Exactly right
- Example: "You hit the nail on the head with that answer."

### Let the cat out of the bag

- Meaning: Reveal a secret
- Example: "I let the cat out of the bag about the surprise party."

## Everyday Expressions

### Of course

- "Of course I can help you."

### Actually

- "Actually, I prefer tea."

### By the way

- "By the way, did you call John?"

### I mean

- "I mean, it's not that difficult."

### It depends

- "It depends on the situation."

### Sounds good

- "That sounds good to me."

### That's right

- "That's right, I agree."

### I'm not sure

- "I'm not sure about that."

### I don't think so

- "I don't think so, but let me check."

### No way

- "No way! That's amazing."

### Take it easy

- "Take it easy and rest."

### Keep in touch

- "Let's keep in touch."

## Usage Tips

- Learn in context
- Don't translate literally
- Use in appropriate situations
- Practice with native speakers
