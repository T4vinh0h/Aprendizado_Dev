# Advanced Grammar

## Past Perfect

### Formation

Subject + had + past participle

### Usage

- Actions completed before another past action
- Earlier past events

### Examples

- I had finished when he arrived.
- She had already left when we called.
- They had studied before the test.

## Future Perfect

### Formation

Subject + will have + past participle

### Usage

- Actions completed by a specific future time
- Future predictions about completion

### Examples

- I will have finished by tomorrow.
- She will have graduated by 2027.
- They will have completed the project by next month.

## Past Perfect Continuous

### Formation

Subject + had been + verb + -ing

### Usage

- Actions in progress before another past action
- Emphasis on duration

### Examples

- I had been working for 5 years when I got promoted.
- She had been studying before she fell asleep.
- They had been waiting for hours when the bus arrived.

## Future Continuous

### Formation

Subject + will be + verb + -ing

### Usage

- Actions in progress at a specific future time
- Future plans and arrangements

### Examples

- I will be working at this time tomorrow.
- She will be traveling next week.
- They will be meeting at 3 PM.

## Future Perfect Continuous

### Formation

Subject + will have been + verb + -ing

### Usage

- Emphasis on duration up to a future time
- Actions continuing until a future point

### Examples

- I will have been working here for 10 years next month.
- She will have been studying for 6 hours by tonight.
- They will have been waiting for 2 hours when the train arrives.

## Passive Voice

### Formation

Subject + be + past participle (+ by agent)

### Usage

- When the action is more important than the doer
- When the doer is unknown
- Formal writing

### Examples

- The project was completed by the team.
- This code was written by a developer.
- The website is being updated.

## Reported Speech

### Direct to Indirect

- Present → Past
- Past → Past Perfect
- Will → Would
- Can → Could

### Examples

- Direct: "I am working," he said.
- Indirect: He said that he was working.

- Direct: "I will help," she promised.
- Indirect: She promised that she would help.

## Relative Clauses

### Defining

- Essential information
- No commas
- Use that/who/which

### Non-defining

- Extra information
- Commas
- Use who/which (not that)

### Examples

- The man who called me is my boss.
- My car, which is red, is in the garage.

## Gerunds and Infinitives

### Gerunds (verb + -ing)

- After certain verbs: enjoy, avoid, mind
- As subjects: Swimming is fun.
- After prepositions: I'm good at cooking.

### Infinitives (to + verb)

- After certain verbs: want, need, decide
- After adjectives: It's important to study.
- To express purpose: I study to learn.

## Mixed Conditionals

### Past action, present result

If + past perfect, would + verb

- If I had studied harder, I would be fluent now.

### Present state, past result

If + past simple, would have + past participle

- If I were rich, I would have bought that house.

## Inversion

### Negative adverbs at the beginning

- Never have I seen...
- Rarely do we...
- Seldom does he...

## Emphasis

### Cleft sentences

- It was John who called.
- What I need is time.

## Subjunctive

### Hypothetical situations

- If I were you...
- I suggest that he be...
- It's important that she study...
